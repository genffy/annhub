一段关于机会成本的短文

Every decision carries an opportunity cost: the value of the best alternative forgone. When a central bank tightens policy, the opportunity cost of holding money rises, and asset prices adjust accordingly. Investors who ignore this trade-off often discover, belatedly, that the safest-looking choice was the most expensive one.

In practice, framing decisions around opportunity costs forces a discipline that raw forecasts cannot provide: it asks not whether an idea is good, but whether it is better than the next best use of the same capital, the same attention, and the same time.
