Why retries need jitter

Jane Doe · Reliability · 6 min read

When a dependency slows down, every client that times out comes back for a second try — and those retries land on a service that has not recovered. The fix is not to retry less often, but to stop retrying in unison: wait longer after each failure and add a random amount on top, so a fleet of clients spreads its retries instead of arriving as one wave.

Measure it before you argue about it

We switched the payments client from fixed 250 ms retries to exponential backoff with full jitter and watched the retry queue during the next brownout. The peak dissolved; the dependency came back a minute earlier; nobody wrote a sad postmortem paragraph about retry storms.

The two lines that matter

const wait = min(cap, base * 2 ** attempt)
const delay = wait * Math.random()      // full jitter

base × 2^n grows the ceiling; multiplying by a fresh Math.random() spreads the fleet across the whole interval instead of its floor. Cap the ceiling, or a long outage will out-wait your user's patience.

A retry is not free. It is a loan against capacity you hope still exists.

Backoff with jitter shipped behind a flag two weeks ago and is now the default for every internal client. The dashboards above update live; the next brownout is somebody else's calm Tuesday.
